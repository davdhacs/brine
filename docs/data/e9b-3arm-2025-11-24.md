# E9 three-arm comparison: fresh commits, ephemeral runners (2025-11-24)

10 commits of curl master (master~10 → master~1, chronological), each
arm in its own repo (separate 10GB cache quota), same runner pool,
calibration probe + cpuinfo recorded per run. The curl build mirrors
one linux.yml job (mbedtls dep + autotools build).

## Medians (curl build phase only)

| arm | strategy | median | range | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | actions/cache (incumbent) | 34.0s | 23.6-43.6s | 10 |
| B | ccache through brine serve | 32.2s | 0.0-49.3s | 10* |
| C | receipt + mtime incremental | **21.8s** | 7.0-49.3s | 10 |

*arm B contaminated: serve-step bug fixed mid-batch; 0.0s entries are
whole-command receipt hits from earlier validation, not ccache hits.

Cold 49.3s to incremental median 21.8s = **2.3x sustained** across the
full chain, limited by curl's --enable-unity (all sources in one
compile unit; F15).

## Key findings

- Arm C (receipts + mtime normalization) beats the incumbent by ~36%
  on median fresh-commit builds - without ccache, without any build
  system adoption beyond 5 lines of .brine.json + the mtime normalizer.
- Arm B (ccache through serve) showed NO benefit in this batch - the
  serve-step bug (trailing-space no-op in the workflow patch) meant
  ccache ran local-only for most runs. The 3 clean ccache runs
  (29.7-49.3s) are comparable to arm A. Needs a clean re-run.
- The per-commit diff size is the dominant variable for arm C:
  1-file diffs -> 7s, 9-file diffs -> 25s. Unity builds cap the floor.
